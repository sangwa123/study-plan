🎯 **The EdTech Lesson Rwanda is Teaching the World**

Over the past 24 hours, Rwanda's education ecosystem just proved something crucial: *Building for teachers doesn't mean building without them.*

Here's what's happening:

A Mastercard Foundation EdTech Fellowship (just launched) is reshaping how innovators approach education technology in Rwanda. And the pattern is clear:

❌ Don't design a "solution" then find a school willing to use it
✅ Start in real classrooms. Listen to teachers. Let *them* tell you what's broken.

**Why this matters for Africa's tech future:**

Most EdTech startups fail because they optimize for what looks impressive, not what improves learning outcomes. Rwanda's Basic Education Board is literally saying "no" to innovations until founders go back and co-design with schools.

One startup came in confident. Left needing a complete redesign. Then came back with something teachers actually wanted. That's the friction that builds real products.

**The bigger play:**

- Carnegie Mellon's Learning Sciences program is embedding this philosophy
- Rwanda TVET Board treating schools as *partners*, not customers

This isn't just good for Rwanda. It's a blueprint for how Africa builds sustainably — not by importing solutions, but by *designing* them locally, with local voices.

The most innovative education tech company won't be the one with the slickest interface. It'll be the one willing to listen first.
